# Deterministic CEM replay audit v1

The task optimizer is deterministic only after three things are fixed:

1. optimizer configuration and optimizer seed;
2. numerical runtime identity;
3. the candidate objective values measured during training.

The red-team stack now treats those as separate evidence layers rather than collapsing them into a generic claim of reproducibility.

## Layer A: execution-budget reconstruction

`fly-sniff-redteam-budget` reconstructs how many candidate evaluations and episode evaluations are represented by the persisted artifact. It verifies frozen training seed membership, generation counts, candidate receipt counts, and full-pool evaluation counts.

This answers:

> Does the artifact represent the same amount of optimization work for intact, rewired, and lesioned topologies?

It does not prove the candidate parameter vectors were actually generated by the frozen CEM RNG.

## Layer B: optimizer-history binding

The official runtime-sealed training artifact stores:

```text
optimizer_history_sha256
```

That hash covers the complete generation history, including seed batches, candidate receipts, objectives, best candidates, and distribution updates. The hash is included in the training audit receipt alongside graph, config, seed, parameter, compute-budget, and numerical-runtime hashes.

This answers:

> Has the persisted optimizer history changed since the promotable training artifact was sealed?

## Layer C: deterministic CEM mechanics replay

`fly-sniff-redteam-cem-replay` regenerates the optimizer mechanics under the exact sealed numerical runtime.

For every generation it independently regenerates:

- the common-random-number training seed batch;
- the log-space Gaussian candidate population;
- clipping to frozen parameter bounds;
- the forced current-mean candidate at population index 0;
- every decoded parameter set and SHA-256;
- deterministic objective tie-breaking;
- elite membership;
- population and elite objective summaries;
- best-candidate identity and parameters;
- updated distribution mean;
- updated log-space sigma.

After the final generation it independently regenerates the trained parameter set and verifies its SHA-256.

The only generation-level values replay accepts as inputs from the persisted history are the candidate objective scores themselves. Those scores determine elite selection and therefore later generations.

## What replay proves

A passing replay supports the statement:

> Given the frozen task-optimization config, optimizer seed, sealed numerical runtime, and the recorded candidate objective values, the persisted CEM trajectory and final parameter set are reproducible.

That is substantially stronger than simply recording an optimizer seed.

## What replay does not prove

A passing replay does **not** independently establish that:

- each candidate objective was honestly measured from the claimed simulator episodes;
- no external or discarded optimization runs occurred;
- a person never inspected final-test outcomes;
- the biological role assignment is correct;
- the rewire ensemble is a stationary graph null;
- navigation performance supports a biological claim.

Objective recomputation from episode-level receipts would be a further audit layer. The one-way final benchmark remains a separate experimental step.

## Scientific rule

CEM replay is a provenance/integrity check, not a navigation-performance threshold. A replay failure invalidates the training artifact because its stated deterministic provenance cannot be reproduced. A replay success does not promote the biological claim by itself.
